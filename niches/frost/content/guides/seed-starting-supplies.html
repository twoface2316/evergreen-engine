The essentials

You can start seeds with very little. These are the pieces that make the biggest difference:

  • {{gear:seed-trays|Cell trays with a humidity dome}}. Cell trays give each seedling its own root space. Use them inside a solid tray with no drainage holes so you can water from below. The clear dome holds humidity while seeds germinate; take it off once most have sprouted.
  • {{gear:seed-mix|Seed-starting mix}}. A fine, sterile mix, usually peat or coir with vermiculite and no real soil. University of Minnesota Extension recommends these commercial mixes for starting seeds. Garden soil compacts in small containers and can carry disease.
  • A light. A sunny window is rarely enough in late winter. A plain LED shop light or a {{gear:grow-light|grow light}} hung a few inches above the seedlings is the most important purchase on this list. See how to choose a grow light.
  • Labels and a marker. Tomato and pepper seedlings look identical. {{gear:plant-labels|Plant labels}} save a lot of guessing in May.

Upgrades worth buying

  • {{gear:heat-mat|Seedling heat mat}}. Peppers, tomatoes, and eggplant germinate fastest in soil around 75–85°F. University of Minnesota Extension notes that potting mix can run about 5°F cooler than the room, so a mat makes a real difference for warm-season crops. Get one with a thermostat if you can.
  • {{gear:outlet-timer|Outlet timer}}. Seedlings need 12–16 hours of light and a dark period each night. A timer handles it so you don't have to.
  • {{gear:clip-fan|Small fan}}. A gentle breeze for a few hours a day builds sturdier stems and helps prevent damping-off, the fungal disease that topples seedlings at the soil line.
  • {{gear:nursery-pots|Larger pots for potting up}}. Tomatoes and peppers often outgrow their cells before planting day. Moving them into 3–4 inch pots keeps roots from circling.

What you can skip

  • All-in-one "seed starting systems" with built-in lights. They're convenient but usually cost more than a tray, a shop light, and a timer bought separately, and the lights are often weaker.
  • Fertilizer at the start. Seeds carry their own food. Start feeding at half strength once true leaves appear.
  • Garden soil or bagged potting soil for germination. Fine for potting up later, too coarse and heavy for seeds.

Timing is the other half

Good supplies can't save seedlings started at the wrong time. Each crop counts back a set number of weeks from your last spring frost: onions and peppers first, tomatoes a few weeks later, squash and melons last. See when to start seeds indoors, or look up your ZIP code for dates calculated for your town.

Before planting out

Seedlings raised indoors need a week or more of gradual outdoor exposure first. A {{gear:cold-frame|cold frame}} makes that easier. See how to harden off seedlings.
