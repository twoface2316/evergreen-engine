What PFAS are

Per- and polyfluoroalkyl substances are thousands of industrial chemicals used since the 1940s in nonstick coatings, stain- and water-resistant fabrics, food packaging and firefighting foam. Their carbon-fluorine bonds make them extremely persistent — hence "forever chemicals" — and they reach drinking water from factories, airports and military bases that used firefighting foam, landfills and wastewater. Long-term exposure to some PFAS has been linked to certain cancers, effects on the immune system, and developmental effects.

Where the federal rules stand

In April 2024, EPA set the first national drinking water limits for PFAS:

  • PFOA and PFOS: 4 parts per trillion each.
  • PFHxS, PFNA and HFPO-DA ("GenX"): 10 parts per trillion each, plus a combined "hazard index" limit for mixtures of PFHxS, PFNA, GenX and PFBS.

Utilities have to complete initial PFAS monitoring by 2027 and originally had to meet the limits by 2029.

In May 2026, EPA proposed two changes: keeping the PFOA and PFOS limits but letting qualifying utilities extend the compliance deadline two years, to 2031, and rescinding the limits for PFHxS, PFNA, GenX and the mixture rule. As of October 2026 those are proposals, not final rules, and legal challenges to the 2024 standards are still pending. Several states set their own PFAS limits, some stricter than the federal ones.

Because compliance isn't due yet, PFAS generally don't show up as violations in EPA's records — including the violation histories on this site. A clean record doesn't mean PFAS-free water.

How to find your utility's PFAS results

  • Your water quality report. Many utilities now list PFAS results, especially where they've been detected.
  • EPA's UCMR 5 data. Under the Fifth Unregulated Contaminant Monitoring Rule, utilities serving 3,300 or more people (and a sample of smaller ones) tested for 29 PFAS between 2023 and 2025. EPA publishes the results by water system in its UCMR 5 data finder.
  • Your state's drinking water program, which may have tested more systems or set state limits.
  • Private wells near known sources should be tested by a lab certified for PFAS methods; home test strips aren't sensitive enough at parts-per-trillion levels.

Filters that remove PFAS

Two home technologies work well:

  • Reverse osmosis removes the widest range of PFAS, including the short-chain compounds carbon handles less well. Look for NSF/ANSI 58 certification listing PFOA and PFOS reduction. Example: {{amazon:reverse osmosis system NSF 58 PFAS|NSF 58 reverse osmosis systems}}.
  • Activated carbon filters certified under NSF/ANSI 53 for PFOA and PFOS reduction — including some pitchers, faucet mounts and under-sink units. Change cartridges on schedule; saturated carbon stops working.
